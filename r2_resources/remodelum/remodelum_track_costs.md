## The System: How to Track Your Renovation Cost and Budget

There are 3 numbers you must track:

### 1\. Your Budget: What you are planning to spend

Before spending a single dollar, homeowners should define [**a structured budget**](/renovation-estimating-guide)**.** The Budget is a top-down number that will help you plan your finances and figure out the scope of work (am I willing to spend the amount for this work?):

**Professional Services Costs**

These expenses cover **labor and expertise** from professionals such as general contractors, architects, designers, and specialized trades (e.g., electricians, plumbers).

**Purchased Items and Materials**

Beyond professional services, homeowners must also track **physical purchases**, such as furniture, appliances, lighting, and materials.

### 2\. Accepted costs: What you’ve agreed to pay (contracts, planned purchases).

While the budget is a top-down number, as you move along with your renovation you must track the costs you are agreeing or committing to spend on.

-   For professional services, those include accepted quotes/ contracts and change orders
    
-   For purchases, those include items you are planning to buy (such as furniture or appliances)
    

These values will always inform you of the actual confirmed cost of your renovation. At the end of your renovation, you want these costs to be as close as possible to your original budget.

### 3\. Actual payments: What you’ve agreed to pay (contracts, planned purchases).

Once the accepted costs are in place, it’s time to **track actual payments**. In an ideal world, payments would match the accepted costs exactly—but in reality, **deviations are inevitable**.

-   **Professional services costs may change due to change orders**—unplanned adjustments to the scope of work. These should be **documented, priced, and approved** before payment is made. As a change order is accepted, this should be recorded as a committed cost.
    
-   **Purchased item costs may deviate due to price fluctuations** or last-minute additions. For example, a homeowner may find a discounted couch or upgrade appliances mid-renovation, leading to a price shift.
    

By consistently **logging all payments and comparing them to the committed cosrs**, you will **spot discrepancies early** and adjust accordingly.

## Choosing the Right Tools for Cost Tracking

Homeowners have several options when it comes to tracking renovation expenses, each with **its pros and cons**.

### 1\. Traditional Methods: Spreadsheets or Pen and Paper

Some homeowners prefer to track expenses manually using **Excel spreadsheets** or simple notebooks. While these methods **offer flexibility**, they have **major limitations**—they require constant updates, lack built-in automation, and fail to store **supporting documents** like receipts and invoices. Over time, tracking expenses manually can become **messy and difficult to manage**.

### 2\. Hiring a Project Manager for Financial Oversight

Some contractors offer **financial tracking as part of their project management services**. While this can be useful for large-scale renovations, these services tend to be **expensive** and are **only as reliable as the person managing them**. Since many project managers specialize in **construction rather than financial oversight**, their records may still **lack precision and organization**.
